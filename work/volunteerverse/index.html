CS 147L · Mobile App Development

VolunteerVerse

A mobile app that brings local organizations and volunteers together in one place, built with React Native and Supabase.
View GitHub Repo
My Role UX Design, Feature Scoping, Frontend & Backend
Timeline Fall 2025
Team 3-Person Team
Built With React Native, Expo Go, Supabase

Motivation

Finding ways to volunteer in your own neighborhood shouldn't require detective work. Usually, people looking to help out have to piece together information from across Instagram stories, local Facebook posts, and broken or outdated nonprofit websites.

On the other side, local organizations often struggle to get the word out and keep track of who has committed to helping. We built VolunteerVerse so both sides could meet in one place: organizations can create profiles, post upcoming events, and track signups, and volunteers can discover causes they care about, sign up for shifts, and keep track of their volunteering schedule.

My contribution

Working on a three-person team for CS 147L, I contributed across design and engineering. I started by scoping out our core features and designing the initial app screens, then spent most of the project building the React Native frontend and hooking it up to our Supabase database.

I built the login, authentication, and onboarding flows so individual volunteers and local organizations could set up and manage their profiles.

I built the real-time event creation and registration system. When an organization posts a new volunteer opportunity, it immediately appears in the app for volunteers to view. Volunteers can sign up with a tap and view all of their upcoming shifts in one place.

Focusing on What Matters

With a small team and 4 weeks to build, our biggest challenge was deciding where to spend our time.

We began development with the idea that the main screen should be a map so people can find opportunities near them. When we ran into financial limits with our chosen map APIs, we decided to spend that time elsewhere instead. By the end of the four weeks, an organizer could post a shift and a volunteer could see it, sign up, and find it on their schedule.
