Stanford Virtual Human Interaction Lab (VHIL) · Honors Research

Honors Thesis

The Impact of Spatiality and Modality on Group Communications in Extended Reality Teleconferencing
Read Full Paper (PDF) ↗ Stanford Repository (DOI) ↗ View Presentation & Slides
Role Lead Researcher (End-to-End Ownership)
Timeline 12 Months (2025–2026)
Scale & Leadership 150 Participants · Trained 5 Research Assistants
Stack & Methods Apple Vision Pro, Python, Multi-Modal Telemetry
01 · Context

The Question

As enterprise and consumer hardware leaders invest heavily in Extended Reality (XR) teleconferencing, product teams have largely operated under a core assumption: that maximizing spatial realism and environmental immersion automatically yields superior remote collaboration.

Conducted at the Stanford Virtual Human Interaction Lab (VHIL), this study empirically tested how photorealistic avatars (Apple Vision Pro Personas), spatial vs. non-spatial (grid) layouts, and passthrough vs. fully virtual environments impact group communication across groups of 2, 3, and 4 participants.

02 · Execution

My Role & Process

I drove this 12-month research initiative from initial ideation to completion, managing both the operational complexity of hardware-based human-subjects testing and the technical engineering required to process the data:

Study & Operations Management: Authored the experimental protocols, developed quantitative and qualitative measures, recruited 150 participants, and trained a team of 5 research assistants to assist with hardware calibration and recording workflows. I personally facilitated every participant session—guiding cohorts through Apple Vision Pro Persona capture before placing them into controlled Spatial or Non-Spatial FaceTime calls across Passthrough or Virtual Environments.

Multi-Modal Data Engineering: Built custom data extraction and analysis utilities in Python to synthesize a complex, multi-modal dataset comprising validated multiple-choice and free-response questionnaires, transcribed participant speech, external room recordings, and first-person headset POV video. The completed thesis earned Departmental Honors and serves as the foundation for an upcoming lab publication.

03 · Impact

Product & UX Takeaways

Key Finding for XR Product Teams

“For developers and enterprise adopters of XR teleconferencing, these findings necessitate a critical re-evaluation of design priorities. The current industry trajectory assumes that maximizing spatial realism and environmental immersion results in better collaborative experiences. However, the data reveal that spatial configurations do not automatically mitigate the cognitive or physical toll of remote interactions. In practice, forcing users into three-dimensional spatial arrangements may actually increase social friction compared to standard grid layouts. Teleconferencing platforms should reconsider the push to deprecate traditional two-dimensional grid views, as these structures provide a safe, established social boundary that users may prefer when navigating the complexities of multi-person interactions in less familiar modalities like VR.”

Rather than treating 2D grid layouts as legacy compromises to be phased out, XR product designers should preserve familiar interaction boundaries as user-selectable defaults—allowing teams to opt into spatial arrangements only when the collaborative task specifically benefits from 3D positioning.
