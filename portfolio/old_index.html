Research & scholarship

May 2025 – June 2026

Honors Thesis

In Stanford’s Virtual Human Interaction Lab, I brought groups of two, three, or four people into FaceTime on Apple Vision Pro. Callers saw one another’s photorealistic avatars either in a grid or in three-dimensional space. A second condition varied whether participants could see their physical surroundings.

I wrote the protocols, recruited 133 participants, trained five research assistants, and ran each session. While spatial conversations looked beneficial from the outside, comparing attention across conditions did not support this impression.

Fall 2025

The Embodied Interface

Karan Soin and I surveyed biosensing and haptics in extended reality and proposed ways to combine these technologies in future interfaces.

I wrote the abstract, the survey of current technology, and the proposals for future systems. I also examined their technical requirements, theoretical capabilities and limitations, and implications for individuals and society.

The Party

A narrative VR artwork following a Stanford afterparty and the consequences of alcohol abuse.

I built and voiced the experience in Unity. Critiques pushed me beyond recreating a familiar room toward the feelings I remembered most clearly, especially the anxiety of hearing a friend get sick.

Spring 2026

Myodyssey

A woodland lemming is an unusual protagonist for an adventure game. Our five-person team wanted the player to experience habitat loss from the position of a small animal trying to survive it. I developed the concept as a browser game told through text, illustrations, and sound.

I designed and coded the opening level, including its interactive passages, inventory, and vegetable stew puzzle, and composed the five-track soundtrack. Across nine playtests, we saw people reread passages while missing clickable words. We grayed out visited text and marked interactive objects so players could spend more of their attention on the story.

Fall 2024

T.AI

In interviews with four educators and two students, our team heard versions of the same problem from opposite sides of the classroom. Students wanted feedback they could act on. Teachers wanted to give it, but had little time to respond to every assignment individually. Our mobile prototype lets a teacher photograph a submission and review feedback generated against their own rubric.

I helped develop the product concept, built low- and medium-fidelity prototypes, and worked on the poster and report. One consequential change came during evaluation: annotations layered over handwriting crowded the phone screen. Giving the feedback its own space below the image let teachers read it alongside the original work.

Fall 2025

VolunteerVerse

Volunteer opportunities in a neighborhood might be spread across Instagram stories, community groups, and individual organizations’ sites. Our three-person team built a mobile app where an organizer could post an event, a volunteer could register, and both could see the resulting schedule.

I scoped the features, designed the initial screens, and connected our React Native frontend to Supabase. I implemented account setup for both roles and real-time event registration. With four weeks to build, we dropped our planned map after running into API limitations and focused on making the entire path from posting to signup work.

Creative practice

2022–2026

Stanford Harmonics

During four years as publicity director for the Stanford Harmonics, I designed six show posters and accompanying programs, developed audition campaigns, and redesigned the group’s website. Each concert needed a visual identity of its own. For auditions, the challenge was making a group with an experimental rock repertoire feel approachable to someone hearing us for the first time.

The website also served listeners and people looking to book us. I brought albums, videos, and streaming embeds together on one Music page. For the group’s recordings, I arranged “Big Shot” and performed the solo and worked as a recording engineer on “Deep Blue.” Both recordings are work-in-progress mixes edited by Parthiv Krishna and mixed by Ed Boyer. The case study includes the website comparison alongside the print campaigns, so you can see how the group’s identity carried across a screen, a poster, and a concert program.

Critical & interpretive scholarship

Spring 2025

Delicate or Daunting?

An art history paper on homoeroticism in Michelangelo’s David and its Florentine contemporaries, written while studying in Florence.

I compared the figures’ ages, poses, and scale to ask how Michelangelo changed the relationship between David and the viewer. My photographs let me ground that argument in details I could examine in person.

Spring 2025

Understanding Rocky Horror

A film studies presentation on how The Rocky Horror Picture Show introduces queer experience to a straight audience through Brad and Janet’s changing perspective.

I traced the move from observation to participation through camera angles, musical numbers, and repeated scenes. The sample pairs my original slides with a lightly edited transcript of my speaker notes.
